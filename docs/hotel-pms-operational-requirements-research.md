# Hotel PMS operational requirements and pain points

**Research date:** 2026-11-15

**Purpose:** Establish a grounded requirements baseline for extending this repository from a room-type booking-request engine into a usable hotel property-management system (PMS).
**Evidence:** First-party Oracle Hospitality OPERA Cloud, Mews, and Cloudbeds operational documentation; applicable primary standards/regulations. Requirements below are a synthesis, not a claim that every property needs every feature.

## Executive summary

A hotel PMS needs to keep one reliable operational record from the first availability enquiry through reservation changes, arrival, in-house service, checkout, settlement, and room turnaround. The central risks are conflicting inventory, staff working from different room states, missed arrival/departure work, unposted or misallocated charges, and unreconstructable changes. A room-type calendar alone cannot safely answer the staff question “which physical room is vacant, clean, inspected, sellable, and ready for this arrival?”

The broadest cross-vendor baseline is: reservation lifecycle and availability; assignment of physical units; front-desk arrival/departure workflows; room/housekeeping/maintenance states; a guest folio with attributable charges, payments, adjustments, and a printable/exportable statement; role-controlled staff actions; operational and financial reporting; and a dependable daily close. Property-specific policies and jurisdiction rules must be configurable. OTA distribution, payment processing, POS, door locks, ID scanning, and messaging are integrations or later phases, not prerequisites for a single-property core.

## Operational pain points a PMS should remove

These are operational problems inferred from the documented workflows, not survey claims:

1. **Overbooking and stale availability:** inventory can be reserved through several channels or edited by multiple staff. Availability and restrictions therefore need a single authoritative source, atomic allocation, and prompt propagation to any connected sales channels. Oracle’s distribution documentation describes publishing real-time availability, sell limits, and restrictions and exposes overbooking-protection controls ([Availability Management](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/integration-platform/crsig/c_4_availability_management.htm), [Overbooking Protection](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud-distribution/25.5/ohdig/ch_overbooking_protection.htm)).
2. **A reservation is not the same as a room being ready:** an occupied/vacant state and a clean/dirty/inspected state answer different questions. Cloudbeds explicitly keeps housekeeping condition separate from inventory availability; Mews requires inspection before check-in in its configured workflow ([Cloudbeds room conditions](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/216540808-Housekeeping-room-conditions), [Mews space status](https://help.mews.com/s/article/change-a-space-s-status?language=en_US)). A system that conflates them can show a vacant but dirty room as ready.
3. **Front desk and housekeeping communicate by interruption:** without a shared, current board, reception must call or message staff to learn readiness, and work can be duplicated or missed. Mews describes how delayed status updates can leave clean rooms unavailable for arriving guests; Oracle supports task assignment, room forecasts, discrepancy handling, and maintenance requests ([Mews space status report](https://help.mews.com/s/article/space-status-report), [Oracle room management](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/25.2/ocsuh/c_housekeeping_room_management.htm)).
4. **Arrival/departure exceptions get lost:** late arrival, early departure, no-show, cancellation, extension, room move, and room change affect both inventory and what staff must do next. First-party PMS guides include explicit cancellation reasons, restoration/history, no-show handling, and departure search rather than treating a booking as a single static row ([Oracle cancellation](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/24.3/ocsuh/t_managing_reservation_cancellation.htm), [Mews no-shows](https://help.mews.com/s/article/how-do-no-shows-work)).
5. **The guest bill is incomplete or difficult to reconcile:** room nights, taxes/fees, restaurant/minibar/service postings, deposits, payments, rebates, and corrections can be split among payers or accounts. PMS billing supports multiple folio windows, charge routing, transfer/adjustment, payment reversal, and final folio output ([Oracle billing](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/26.4/ocsuh/c_manage_billing_manage_billing.htm)).
6. **“Today” becomes ambiguous overnight:** operational date, stay/service date, transaction timestamp, and calendar date can differ. Night audit posts scheduled charges, applies configured no-show/extension transitions, advances the business date, and supports daily reconciliation. Closed-day totals should not silently change ([Cloudbeds Night Audit](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/31030047512347-Night-Audit-in-Cloudbeds-PMS-Overview-and-Key-Features), [business/service/transaction dates](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/57812137970971-Understand-Stay-Date-Service-Date-Business-Date-and-Transaction-Date-in-Cloudbeds-Reports)).
7. **Staff cannot explain who changed a price, room state, payment, or booking:** sensitive actions require permissions and a durable history with actor, time, target, old/new values, and reason where appropriate. Mews’ action log and Oracle’s logged role changes illustrate the operational audit trail expectation ([Mews action log](https://help.mews.com/s/article/action-log?language=en_US), [Oracle Role Manager guide](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/26.1/ocsuh/G48603_02.pdf)).

## Requirements baseline

### Core for a single-property PMS

| Area | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Property and sellable inventory | Configure property timezone, business date/check-in and checkout times, room types, physical rooms, capacity/occupancy, amenities, active state, and date-specific sellable capacity/rates. Calculate availability across the full stay, treating checkout as exclusive. Support restrictions or clearly declare them out of scope. Do not sell out-of-order rooms; keep “out of service” treatment configurable because products differ on whether these units count against inventory. |
| Reservation lifecycle | Create a reservation with a durable reference, guest/contact, party size, dates, room type/quantity, rate/price snapshot, source, status, and notes. Support inquiry/request, optional/held, confirmed, checked-in, checked-out, canceled, and no-show states as the property’s policy requires. Allow staff to search/filter arrivals, departures, in-house, canceled, and no-show bookings. Record reason/history; cancellation releases inventory and can trigger a configured fee or deposit rule. Support edits, extension, early departure, room moves, and linked/group stays without losing the audit or revenue history. |
| Room assignment and front desk | Assign a specific room before or at check-in, see conflicts and readiness, record actual arrival/departure, validate party and required details, and prevent assignment of unavailable or not-ready rooms according to policy. Support walk-in/manual reservations, room changes, early/late exceptions, and operational notes. Check-in/out must update reservation occupancy and physical-room state consistently. |
| Room status board | Show every physical room with at least separate occupancy/front-desk and housekeeping dimensions, upcoming reservation context, and sellable/out-of-service/out-of-order status. Filter by floor, room/type, state, arrival/departure, and assignment. Make empty, occupied, arriving, departing, dirty, clean, inspected, and maintenance-blocked rooms quickly distinguishable without color alone. Resolve front-office/housekeeping occupancy discrepancies instead of silently overwriting them. |
| Housekeeping and maintenance | Mark rooms dirty, clean, and (if used) inspected; assign rooms/tasks to staff; prioritize arrivals/stayovers/departures; record completion and notes. Prevent check-in until the configured readiness state. Record out-of-order/out-of-service periods with date range, reason, owner, and return state; optionally attach evidence. Track maintenance issues separately from cleaning. Oracle documents distinct clean/dirty/inspected/pickup and OOS/OOO states; OOO removes inventory while OOS can remain sellable in its documented configuration ([Oracle Housekeeping Board](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/24.3/ocsuh/t_housekeeping_using_the_housekeeping_board.htm)). |
| Guest folio and invoice/statement | Maintain an append-only transaction ledger per stay/account: room-night charges; configured taxes/fees; service/add-on charges with item, quantity, service date, price, and source; deposits/payments; discounts/rebates; adjustments/refunds/voids; balance and payer. Permit authorized corrections with reason and history rather than erasing settled financial activity. Preview and print/export a folio/statement with property and guest details, itemized services, dates, quantities, amounts, taxes/discounts, payments, balance, currency, and invoice identifiers required by local rules. Allow charge transfer/split among guest/company/payers if the property needs it. The exact “tax invoice” format is jurisdiction dependent. |
| Day close and reconciliation | Define property business date and timezone; provide a controlled daily close/night-audit process to post configured nightly room/tax/add-on charges, identify due-outs/no-shows/unbalanced folios, advance the operating date, and produce summaries. Make the process idempotent and recoverable; closed-period corrections should be explicit adjustments on an open date, not invisible rewriting. Night audit time and its automation rules are property-configurable. |
| Staff access and accountability | Authenticate staff; use named accounts and least-privilege roles (e.g., reception, housekeeping, manager/admin, cashier); scope sensitive actions such as rate/capacity changes, check-in/out overrides, posting/voiding charges, refunds, and audit close. Log security/business events with actor and timestamp. Avoid shared accounts. Use secure sessions, CSRF protection where cookie-authenticated, rate limiting/lockout controls, and protected secrets. |
| Guest-data handling and resilience | Collect only operationally necessary guest data; define retention/deletion and access procedures by applicable law; protect data in transit and at rest; restrict exports; avoid real guest data in demos/tests. Maintain tested backups/restore and a useful outage/recovery procedure. The GDPR, where applicable, requires purpose limitation, data minimisation, storage limitation, integrity/confidentiality, and risk-appropriate security, resilience, restoration, and regular testing ([Regulation (EU) 2016/679, Articles 5 and 32](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng/)). |
| Operational visibility | Dashboard at minimum: arrivals, departures, in-house, rooms vacant/occupied, rooms needing cleaning/inspection, OOO/OOS, pending booking requests, overdue/unbalanced departures, and actionable exceptions. Provide search and filters that match front-desk and housekeeping work. Keep lists usable on a tablet/phone for mobile room checks. |

### Common property-specific or later-phase capabilities

- **Channel manager/OTA/CRS distribution:** required when selling through multiple channels; synchronize availability, rates, restrictions, booking creation/change/cancellation, and error status. Add idempotency and conflict handling to prevent duplicates and oversells. Not needed for an initial direct-only property.
- **Rate/revenue rules:** multiple rate plans, weekday/season pricing, occupancy pricing, minimum/maximum stay, closed-to-arrival/departure, packages, promos, and overbooking controls. Needed only to match the property’s sales policy; manual date-specific rates are a valid initial scope.
- **Payments:** deposits, authorization, payment collection, settlement, refunds, reconciliation, and provider webhooks only if the business elects to process them in-system. If card data is involved, use a compliant provider/terminal/tokenized flow and evaluate PCI scope rather than storing card credentials. PCI SSC notes that outsourcing does not eliminate merchant responsibilities, and encryption alone does not generally remove stored cardholder data from scope ([PCI SSC FAQ](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/does-pci-dss-apply-to-merchants-who-outsource-all-payment-processing-operations-and-never-store-process-or-transmit-cardholder-data/), [PCI SSC scope FAQ](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/faqs/1086/)).
- **POS, minibar, PBX, spa, or restaurant:** post itemized sales to the correct guest folio with transaction code, service date, tax treatment, external source, duplicate protection, and an exception queue. Oracle’s interface documentation describes external POS/minibar/PBX postings and configurable mapping/reconciliation ([Oracle property interface](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/26.4/ocsuh/t_admin_interfaces_configuring_property_interfaces.htm)).
- **Door locks/key cards, ID scanning, self-service check-in, email/SMS/WhatsApp, accounting, revenue management, loyalty, housekeeping mobile apps:** useful integrations when supported by the property’s vendors and staffing model, but each adds vendor availability, privacy, security, and support obligations. Oracle treats ID scanning as an integration and external services dependency ([Oracle check-in workflow](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/22.5/ocsuh/t_checking_in_reservations.htm)).
- **Groups/events, multi-property/chain, corporate accounts, travel agents, loyalty, multi-currency, multilingual service, and owner accounting:** only core if the target property model requires them; otherwise defer.

### Jurisdiction-dependent requirements

Tax rates, mandatory invoice numbering/content, fiscal-device/e-invoice integration, tourism levies, guest identity/reporting obligations, deposit/refund/cancellation rules, accessibility rules, labor/shift records, privacy rights/retention periods, and permitted payment rails vary by country and sometimes locality. The app should make policy/tax values configurable and preserve the evidence needed for accounting; do not encode a country’s legal defaults until the deployment jurisdiction and accounting requirements are confirmed. GDPR applies based on its territorial scope, not merely because this product is Persian-language. PCI DSS applies when the system or associated environment stores, processes, transmits, or can affect the security of cardholder data.

## Current repository fit (documented baseline)

The repository’s [PRD](../prd.md) intentionally describes a smaller booking-request engine: one property, room-type inventory by night, staff confirmation/rejection, a temporary hold, no physical room assignment, no housekeeping/maintenance/POS, and no online or offline payment. The current [README](../README.md) likewise documents public room browsing and requests plus staff booking and room-night inventory management.

That scope is a sound foundation for direct room-type bookings, but it is not yet the operational PMS described above. I cross-checked the implementation against the source schema, API modules, web routes, and existing test inventory. The current implementation matches the documented foundation, with the following gaps:

| Area | Current implementation | Gap and priority |
|---|---|---|
| Guest discovery and requests | **Present.** Public hotel/room-type pages, date availability and Toman prices, request submission, idempotency, and staff confirmation/rejection are implemented. Evidence: `apps/api/prisma/schema.prisma` (`RoomType`, `RoomNightInventory`, `Booking`, `BookingNight`); public web pages under `apps/web/src/app/`; API `bookings`, `availability`, `hotel`, and `room-types` modules. | Keep the request/hold flow and connect confirmed requests to physical room allocation. |
| Booking lifecycle | **Partial.** Pending, confirmed, rejected, canceled, and expired states plus staff actions exist. | Add staff-created walk-ins, edits/extensions, no-show, check-in, in-house, early/late checkout, room moves, and final checkout. **P0** |
| Physical inventory and assignment | **Missing.** Capacity is an aggregate count by room type and date; there is no physical `Room` entity or assignment relation. | Add room numbers, room type/floor/features, date-conflict-safe assignments, and a board showing each unit. Derive or reconcile sellable capacity from one clearly defined source of truth. **P0** |
| Front desk operations | **Missing.** Existing staff routes cover booking requests and room-type/date inventory. | Add arrivals, departures and in-house views; readiness and conflict checks; actual arrival/departure timestamps; operational exceptions. **P0** |
| Housekeeping and maintenance | **Missing.** No housekeeping or maintenance domain/API/UI exists. | Track occupancy separately from dirty/clean/inspected state; assign and prioritize tasks; create maintenance blocks with reasons and date ranges; prevent check-in when policy says a room is not ready. **P0** |
| Charges, folios and invoices | **Missing.** The current booking stores a price total and per-night snapshot, but has no transaction ledger, service catalog, folio, payment, settlement, or print artifact. | Add immutable room/service charge and settlement entries, authorized corrections with reasons, balance checks, and printable itemized folios. Keep fiscal invoice fields configurable until jurisdiction requirements are known. **P0** |
| Staff access and audit | **Partial.** `ADMIN` and `RECEPTIONIST`, secure persisted sessions, CSRF/origin checks, and role guards exist. | Add housekeeping/cashier/manager permissions as workflows require, staff account administration, and durable actor/time/reason audit history for operational and financial changes. **P0** |
| Daily close and reports | **Missing.** There is no property business-date/night-audit workflow or occupancy, housekeeping, revenue, and reconciliation reporting. | Add timezone-aware business date, idempotent daily close, exception lists, and operational summaries after check-in/out and folio accounting are stable. **P1** |
| Security, privacy, resilience | **Partial.** Input validation, rate limits, request IDs, credential/session protections, safe error handling, and health/readiness endpoints exist. | Define guest-data retention/access rules and backup/restore operations before real guest use; verify production secrets, deployment, monitoring, and multi-instance job behavior separately. **P1** |
| Automated flow coverage | **Strong for current scope; absent for new domains.** Existing unit, API, database, and Playwright suites cover booking, auth, inventory, and role protections. | Add full guest-to-checkout coverage, including room assignment, readiness, service posting, balance settlement, print output, race conditions, and staff permission boundaries as each ticket lands. **Required per ticket** |
| External integrations | **Intentionally absent.** There is no OTA/channel manager, payment gateway, POS, lock system, ID scan, or notification provider. | Defer until a property/vendor and local operating requirements are selected. Do not store card credentials. **Later / decision-dependent** |

The highest-impact missing domains are therefore confirmed, not inferred from the README alone: physical room records and assignment; stay execution and exception handling; separate housekeeping/maintenance state; a folio ledger and printable itemized bill; more granular staff access and audit history; and daily close/reporting. The existing security and booking-concurrency foundations should be extended rather than replaced. External distribution and local legal/tax behavior remain explicit product decisions.

## Recommended sequencing for the later implementation plan

1. **Foundations:** property operating settings, physical-room records, explicit reservation lifecycle/status transitions, room assignment, role/permission model, and audit-event model.
2. **Front desk + housekeeping:** arrival/departure board, check-in/out, room state dimensions, room readiness enforcement, housekeeping task assignment, maintenance blocks, and reconciliation of room discrepancies.
3. **Billing:** folio transaction ledger, rate/tax snapshots, service/add-on catalog and postings, authorized adjustments, payment recording (manual/offline records first if desired), and printable/exportable folio.
4. **Daily operations:** business date/night audit, exception dashboards, operational and revenue reports, backups/recovery procedures, and end-to-end workflows.
5. **Integrations:** add one at a time after the hotel confirms a real operational need, vendor, jurisdiction, and support model.

Every phase should preserve transaction history, enforce server-side invariants, and be tested across guest, reception, housekeeping, and admin roles. The source PRD requires worktrees and ticket-level acceptance checks; that workflow should be followed for implementation.
